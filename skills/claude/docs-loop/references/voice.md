# Voice — write like a person, for a person

The reader is a farmer or their office manager. They are good at their job and busy; assume no
software vocabulary and no patience for filler. The test for every page: could they follow it on a
phone, standing in a shed, first try?

## Structure

- Say what the page gets them in the first two sentences, in their words ("By the end of this page
  your crew can clock on from the tablet in the shed").
- Task first, then numbered steps, then the edge case. One action per step, named exactly as the
  screen shows it: "tap the green **Clock on** button", never "initiate the time-tracking workflow".
- Explain a term once, where it first appears, in one plain sentence — then just use it.
- Use the glossary term from the owning `CONTEXT.md` and nothing from its `_Avoid_:` lines; several
  of those bans exist because the wrong word once caused a bug.
- Where a rule is legal (award floor, attestation text, record-keeping), state it plainly and link
  the Fair Work source instead of paraphrasing the law.

## Sound human

Write like you would explain it at the kitchen table. It is fine to say "This trips people up" or
"You'll only do this once a year". Contractions are normal. Short sentences carry instructions —
but vary the length; a four-word sentence after a long one is how people actually talk.

Hard bans (these are the tells of machine writing):

- seamless(ly), effortless(ly), simply, robust, leverage, streamline, empower, elevate, unlock,
  supercharge, delve, "dive into", "game-changer", "best-in-class", "in today's fast-paced world",
  "Whether you're X or Y", "It's important to note", "Let's explore".
- The rule of three ("fast, simple, and reliable") — pick the one claim that is true and make it.
- A closing sentence that restates the section it closes.
- Exclamation marks in instructions. One per page at most, in a genuinely good-news spot.
- Every paragraph opening the same way, or every paragraph the same length. Break the rhythm.

Positive habits that read as human: name the actual thing ("the Casual rate under MA000028", not
"the applicable rate"); admit friction where it exists ("Xero takes a minute to show new employees —
that's Xero, not you"); prefer a concrete example over a general claim.

Read the page aloud. Any sentence that sounds like a brochure gets rewritten; any sentence you would
not say to a neighbour gets shortened until you would.
